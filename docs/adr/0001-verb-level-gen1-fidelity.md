# Gen1 fidelity is measured at the verb, not the numbers or the icons

The brief asks for "the same mechanics with variations to make it work better with
e-ink," which does not say what level of sameness is being promised. We decided that
**fidelity is judged at the player-facing verb**: a Gen1 player must recognise every
action they can take — feed a meal, feed a snack, scold, clean, medicate, play, dim the
lamp, check the status. Numbers, timings, internal representation and presentation are
free. A mechanic may only be varied when e-ink or the desk-companion posture makes the
verb impossible or actively bad, and any variation must preserve the loop that verb
served.

## Considered options

- **Loop-level fidelity** — preserve the feedback loops, let the verbs change freely.
  Rejected as too permissive: it licences a pet that plays nothing like a Tamagotchi and
  invites redesign work the project does not need.
- **Icon-level fidelity** — preserve Gen1's eight-icon shell menu as the literal UI
  structure. Rejected because it locks the interface to a 1996 hardware constraint that
  does not apply here, and the Light icon is precisely the one whose translation was in
  question.

## Consequences

The demand rate is already cut roughly tenfold and the lifespan stretched to two weeks,
so **nothing survives numerically verbatim** — the rubric is the only thing that makes
"verbatim" a meaningful claim. Applied across the ten mechanics in issue #3, it produced
five kept verbatim, five varied, and none cut. Future mechanic questions should be judged
against this rubric rather than re-argued from the brief.
